What Is a Loan Against Property?

A Loan Against Property (LAP) is a secured loan in which eligible property is pledged as collateral. The borrower generally retains ownership, subject to the loan agreement and applicable law, while the lender has security over the property.

01

Business Requirements

Eligible borrowers may use the funds for permitted business expenses, expansion, or working capital, subject to lender terms.

02

Personal Financial Needs

Depending on the lender's policy, funds may be used for eligible personal expenses or planned financial commitments.

03

Property-Backed Financing

Loan eligibility may depend on the property's valuation, ownership records, legal status, and the lender's assessment.

Key Factors to Understand

Review the loan terms and risks carefully before pledging your property.

Property Valuation

The lender may arrange a valuation to assess the property's accepted value and determine the eligible loan amount.

Interest and Repayments

Compare interest rates, repayment schedules, applicable fees, and the total amount payable over the loan tenure.

Collateral Risk

Missing repayments can have serious consequences. The lender may enforce its security over the property in accordance with the agreement and law.

Eligibility and Required Documents

Exact requirements vary by lender, loan purpose, applicant profile, and property type.

Common Eligibility Factors

  • Age and residency criteria set by the lender.
  • Stable income and repayment capacity.
  • Credit history and existing financial obligations.
  • Ownership of an eligible property.
  • Acceptable property valuation and legal documentation.

Commonly Requested Documents

  • Identity proof and address proof.
  • Salary slips or business income records.
  • Bank statements and applicable tax returns.
  • Property ownership documents and title records.
  • Additional financial or legal documents requested by the lender.

This information is a general guide, not a guarantee of eligibility or approval. Requirements must be confirmed with the selected lender.

Frequently Asked Questions

Understand the basics before submitting an enquiry.

Can I continue using my property?

In many standard Loan Against Property arrangements, the owner may continue using the property while it serves as collateral. The loan agreement and applicable law determine the rights and restrictions.

How is the eligible loan amount determined?

Lenders commonly consider the property's accepted valuation, permitted loan-to-value limits, the applicant's repayment capacity, credit profile, and their own lending policies.

Can I use the funds for business purposes?

Some lenders permit business use, subject to the loan product's terms and any applicable restrictions. Confirm the permitted purpose before applying.

What happens if I cannot repay the loan?

Default may lead to penalties, credit consequences, and enforcement against the pledged property, subject to the loan agreement and applicable law. Discuss repayment difficulties with your lender promptly.
